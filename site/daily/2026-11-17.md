## 3. LACCO: Local Accumulation with Computation--Communication Overlap

`相似度 3.813` ｜ Daniele Kopyshevskiy \(MLIA\), Benjamin Th\{\\'e\}rien \(Mila, UdeM\), Nicolas Pinto \(MLIA\), Edouard Oyallon \(MLIA\)

**研究问题**：低带宽网络下训练语言模型时，DiLoCo 类局部方法的外层梯度平均与外层更新位于关键路径，造成同步停顿。

**方法**：提出 LACCO 两阶段算法，将全局通信与本地计算重叠，各副本共享同一全局聚合更新且外层更新不陈旧，并给出收敛性理论保证。

**结果与证据**：FineWeb 预训练实验中一致优于 Eager 与 Delayed DiLoCo 基线，0.5B 规模、16 副本、不同通信间隔下稳健，开销可忽略或可完全重叠。

**方向关联**：与 LLM 支线直接相关：语言模型预训练的分布式训练优化；同为分布式场景但无进化计算成分，与 LLM×EC 交互关系较弱。

**代码复现线索**：论文报告实验使用 FineWeb 数据做语言模型预训练，规模至 0.5B 参数、最多 16 个副本；可用正文（仅前言）未给出更多实现或复现细节。


<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> Training language models across low-bandwidth networks requires reducing synchronization overhead. Local optimization methods such as DiLoCo communicate only after several local steps, but outer gradient averaging and the outer update remain on the critical path. Existing computation-communication overlap methods avoid this stall through delayed or replica-local updates, which can degrade convergence or cause replicas to drift. We propose LACCO (Local Accumulation with Computation-Communication Overlap), a two-stage algorithm that overlaps global communication with local computation while applying globally aggregated updates shared by every replica, without introducing staleness in the outer update. We establish convergence guarantees for smooth non-convex objectives. Experiments on language-model pre-training with FineWeb show that LACCO consistently outperforms communication-overlapped baselines such as Eager and Delayed DiLoCo. These gains remain robust as model size scales up to 0.5B parameters and across a range of communication intervals and numbers of replicas up to 16 workers, while incurring only negligible-or fully overlappable-computational or memory overhead, which demonstrates the strength of LACCO.

</details>

## 4. LoRAWeaver: Directional Adapter Routing in LoRA Representation Space

**方向关联**：与 LLM/API 支线直接相关（LLM 高效微调与适配器库复用）；论文本身不涉及进化计算，与 LLM×EC 交叉在文中未体现。

<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> Large language models are expensive to update through full retraining. Parameter-efficient fine-tuning reduces the cost of adaptation, but repeated adaptation creates a growing pool of task-specific LoRA adapters. This pool complicates adapter selection during inference. Existing routing and encoder-based methods often require retraining when new adapters are added, which limits their use in dynamic settings. An ablation confirms the benefit of directional alignment in the adapter-output space.

## 5. ParetoM$^3$: Learning on the Pareto Set under Preference Guidance via Min-Max-Min Optimization

`相似度 3.753` ｜ Pei Tang, Songtao Lu ｜ The Chinese University of Hong Kong

**研究问题**：研究偏好引导的多目标优化：在多个非凸目标的弱Pareto集上最小化预定义的偏好函数。

**方法**：提出罚函数式min-max-min重构（M³问题），将偏好优化与弱Pareto最优性约束统一，并设计单循环一阶算法ParetoM³求驻点。

**结果与证据**：在局部KŁ假设和足够大罚参数下证明得到ε-驻点且近似弱Pareto最优，给出显式非渐近收敛率；合成基准、图像分类、回归与数学推理实验验证了性能。

**代码复现线索**：论文仅说明实验涵盖合成基准、图像分类、回归与数学推理，所提供文本未给出实现、数据或复现细节。


<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> Learning across multiple inherently conflicting objectives requires selecting desirable trade-offs from the Pareto set. In this work, we study preference-guided multi-objective optimization, where a predefined preference function is minimized over the weak Pareto set of multiple nonconvex objectives. Leveraging a merit function that vanishes on the weak Pareto set, we propose a penalty-based min-max-min formulation, termed the M$^3$ problem, which unifies preference optimization and the enforcement of weak Pareto optimality in a single objective. We further develop ParetoM$^3$, a single-loop first-order algorithm for computing stationary solutions of the resulting M$^3$ problem. Under a local Kurdyka-Lojasiewicz assumption, we prove that ParetoM$^3$ finds an $\epsilon$-stationary point of the M$^3$ problem that is also approximately weakly Pareto optimal for the original multi-objective problem when the penalty parameter is sufficiently large, with explicit non-asymptotic convergence rates. We also show that this stationarity notion recovers existing optimality criteria, including approximate preference stationarity and approximate Karush-Kuhn-Tucker conditions, under the same assumptions used in prior works. Experiments on a synthetic benchmark, image classification, regression, and math reasoning demonstrate that ParetoM$^3$ reliably attains preferred weak Pareto optimal solutions while achieving strong practical performance. Code is available at https://github.com/tpgoodboy/ParetoM3.

## 8. Learning monotone game-theoretic linear MPC

`相似度 3.704` ｜ Alberto Bemporad, Tatiana Tatarenko ｜ Technische Universität Darmstadt; IMT School for Advanced Studies Lucca

**研究问题**：如何仅凭运行中多智能体系统的输入输出数据模仿学习博弈论MPC控制律，且无需智能体披露自身目标。

**方法**：以构造性强单调的LQ广义纳什均衡为内层，经双层优化学习GT-MPC权重，并用联合学习的二次Lyapunov罚项促进闭环稳定。

**结果与证据**：作者数值算例显示可较精确重构底层博弈并通过参考优化塑造闭环行为；仅为单一数值示例验证。

**方向关联**：契合较弱：本文属博弈论MPC与变分均衡学习，不含进化计算或LLM；仅多智能体博弈概念与分布式多智能体辅助阅读方向泛相关。

**代码复现线索**：文中说明双层学习问题可用L-BFGS-B求解；提供的文本未给出数据集或实验设置等其他复现细节。


<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> We propose a method to learn a game-theoretic model predictive control (GT-MPC) law by imitation from input and output data of a running multi-agent system. By interpreting the recorded inputs as the first moves of the equilibria of a game played by the agents, we learn the GT-MPC weights under hard input constraints and soft output constraints, without requiring the agents to reveal their objectives. We also impose that the resulting generalized Nash equilibrium (GNE) problem is strongly monotone, which ensures the existence and uniqueness of the equilibrium and, hence, that the learned GT-MPC law is well defined and can be evaluated efficiently online by variational GNE-seeking algorithms for linear-quadratic games. Moreover, strong monotonicity implies that the equilibrium is almost everywhere differentiable with respect to the references and the weights, which enables automatic differentiation for gradient-based optimization. In addition, we promote closed-loop stability of the unconstrained equilibrium feedback by incorporating a penalty that involves a jointly learned quadratic Lyapunov function. Learning a GT-MPC formulation enables us to explain the agents' objectives, reproduce their behavior in simulation in new scenarios, optimize their set-points, design mechanisms, and enforce additional constraints. A numerical example illustrates the approach.
