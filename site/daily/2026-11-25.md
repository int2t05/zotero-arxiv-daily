> Designing what an agent should optimize can be as difficult as optimizing the agent itself. Most BO methods rely on stationary Gaussian processes, implicitly assuming that an objective black-box function exhibits homogeneous smoothness across the input space. <summary>原始摘要</summary>

At every iteration, it picks the operator that best balances expected quality gain against expected energy cost. Under the Algerian grid's carbon intensity, this cuts CO$_2$e emissions by approximately 85% compared with the most energy-hungry baseline.

## 5. Precision-Aware Learning Rate Transfer Across Block Count in LLM Post-Training

`相似度 3.872` ｜ Nan Chen, Soledad Villar, Soufiane Hayou

**研究问题**：LLM 后训练（SFT/DPO/RLVR）对学习率敏感，网格搜索调参在算力与显存上代价高昂。

**方法**：提出 TipSweep：仅训练最后 k0 个块（典型 k0=1）搜索学习率，用缩放律预测训练任意 k 个块乃至全模型的最优学习率。

**结果与证据**：1–7B 模型实验观察到精度相关缩放律：FP32 主权重时最优率约按 k^\{-1\}，BF16 直接更新时约按 k^\{-1/2\}。

**方向关联**：与 LLM 支线契合：可降低 SFT/DPO/RLVR 后训练调参成本；与进化计算主线无直接关联。

**代码复现线索**：摘要称 6.7B 模型两原型：单张 16 GiB 消费级 GPU 可跑搜索；单遍评估全部候选，五候选约省一半 GPU 时。


<details>
<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> The post-training of pretrained large language models, including supervised finetuning (SFT), direct preference optimization (DPO), and reinforcement learning with verifiable reward (RLVR), is very sensitive to the learning rate, and tuning it through grid search is costly in both computation and memory. In this work, we propose \textbf{TipSweep}, a method to transfer the learning rate across the number of trainable transformer blocks. To do so efficiently, we search for the best learning rate by training only the last $k_0$ blocks (in our experiments, typically $k_0=1$), and provide scaling rules that predict the optimal learning rate when training any set of $k$ blocks, including when $k=L$ (the full transformer). Across experiments with models ranging from 1 to 7 billion parameters, we observe that the optimal learning rate follows precision-dependent scaling rules: the optimal rate scales approximately as $k^{-1}$ with float32 (FP32) master weights, but as $k^{-1/2}$ when updates are applied directly to bfloat16 (BF16) weights without a master copy. We explain both rules using an analytical model that accounts for update rounding, under assumptions supported by profiling experiments. Finally, we develop two prototypes of the learning rate search for a 6.7-billion-parameter model. The first fits the search on a single consumer GPU with 16 GiB of memory. The second speeds up the search on a high-end GPU by evaluating all candidate learning rates in a single pass over the data, so that a five-candidate search takes about half the GPU hours of one full-depth ($k=L$) run.

## 96. The Value of Labels in Data Selection for Least Squares

<summary>原始摘要</summary>

> Suppose a selector sees a finite labelled dataset and chooses $n$ of its rows, with repetition. Ordinary least squares is then fitted to the rows that were chosen, without weights, and the excess loss of the fit on the full dataset is measured in units of the optimal loss. Without further assumptions this ratio does not decay with $n$. We examine the problem for $d$ features and rows of normalized leverage at most $\kappa$, and determine or bound the leading constants. We design a simplified optimization problem, which we call a game, that bounds the constant for datasets with exactly fitted frequent rows, then show that its value is at most one half of the gradient energy in several regimes, and that one half is exact along directions in which the frequent rows are rigid, such as an intercept. For grouped designs, in which reshaping amounts to the allocation of a common budget, the constant is $27d/128$ up to an additive constant, compared with $d$ without labels. Nevertheless, one half is not the constant in every dimension: we construct datasets of extremely high dimension and large leverage on which every selection has an excess ratio of at least $(e^{-1/2}-o(1))\,d\kappa/n$, so that labels are worth at most a factor close to $\sqrt e$ there.
